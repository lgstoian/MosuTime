# Accessibility renders

Generated by `tools/contact_sheet.py`; do not edit this file or `hours.png` by
hand. Rebuild both from the repository root with:

```sh
python tools/contact_sheet.py
```

`hours.png` is a 1694x2302 contact sheet: sixteen native 410x502 renders, four
per row, each labelled with the 24 h time it shows. Between them the scenes
cover every clock digit 0-9, the low and the high end of the battery, all
three Wi-Fi states and both the AM and PM themes.

## What is being probed

The numerals are drawn, not typeset: a seven-segment style geometry of 200 px
with a 22 px stroke and a 22 px gap between digits. The questions this sheet
answers are physical, not aesthetic:

- can a person with reduced contrast sensitivity still separate the strokes?
- is the AM/PM difference visible without relying on hue alone?
- does the status row stay secondary to the time?

## Physical size math

- Display: 410 x 502 px on a 2.06 inch (diagonal) panel.
- Panel diagonal: 2.06 x 25.4 = 52.32 mm.
- Diagonal in pixels: sqrt(410^2 + 502^2) = 648.2 px.
- Pixel pitch: 52.32 mm / 648.2 px = **0.0807 mm/px** (about 81 um per pixel,
  roughly 315 ppi).
- Active area: 410 px x 0.0807 mm/px = **33.1 mm wide** and 502 px x 0.0807
  mm/px = **40.5 mm tall**.
- Clock digit box: 200 px x 0.0807 mm/px = **16.1 mm tall**.
- Stroke: 22 px = **1.8 mm**; gap between digits: 22 px = **1.8 mm**.

For scale: a credit card is 0.76 mm thick, so a 16.1 mm numeral is about 21.2
card thicknesses tall.

## Viewing distance

The digits subtend 3.1 degrees at 30 cm, 2.3 degrees at 40 cm and 0.9 degrees
at 1 m. Normal acuity resolves about 1 arcminute, and comfortable reading
starts around 10-15 arcminutes, so the time stays legible well beyond arm's
length.

- 25-35 cm: normal watch-checking distance, the intended viewing range.
- 50 cm: still comfortable; this is where the 200 px digit height pays off.
- 1 m: readable as a clock on a bedside table, not as text.
- 2 m: the shapes are recognisable but individual digits start to blur; do not
  size the layout for this distance.

Judge the sheet at the distance you actually use the watch, not at 100 % zoom
on a monitor. A 410 px wide face shown at 410 screen pixels is roughly three
times the physical size of the watch on a typical 96 ppi monitor.

## Cataract colour shift

Age-related nuclear cataract yellows and browns the lens. The eye loses short-
wavelength (blue) transmission first and scatters the remaining light, which
changes what this face looks like in three ways:

- **Blue and green lose contrast.** Blue pixels look darker and greyer, and
  low-saturation status colours can disappear into the black background. The
  sheet therefore keeps the AM face warm white and the PM face amber; both
  survive the yellow shift, unlike a blue/white pair.
- **Colour is never the only cue.** Battery state also changes the filled
  proportion of the icon, Wi-Fi failure adds a slash, and the minute line is
  always below the hour line. Someone who cannot tell red from green can still
  read the state from shape and position.
- **Scatter costs edge contrast.** Thin light strokes on black bloom and halo.
  That is why the stroke is 1.8 mm instead of a hairline, and why there is 1.8
  mm of black between digits rather than tight kerning.

When reviewing, dim the room a little and step back: cataracts hurt most in
low light with high-glare, thin, saturated-blue content. If a scene is still
readable under those conditions it will be readable on a wrist.
